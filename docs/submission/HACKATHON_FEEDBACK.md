# HackwithHyderabad — Participant Feedback

**Prompt**: *"Did the competition do anything really well, or are there any ways to improve?"*

---

## 1. Short Version (Ideal for standard text boxes, ~150 words)

HackwithHyderabad provided a fantastic, practical challenge. Focusing the problem on persistent memory in conversational AI rather than just another generic chatbot pushed us to think deeply about real-world engineering constraints: cross-session context, customer data isolation, latency, and guardrails against hallucinated actions. Working hands-on with Hindsight Cloud and Groq's high-speed inference was an incredible learning experience that elevated the project from a toy demo to a production-ready application.

As an area for future improvement, providing a standardized evaluation rubric early in the hackathon and explicit guidelines on video submission requirements (e.g., maximum length and hosting platforms) would help participants prioritize features and prepare final submissions more smoothly. Overall, it was an engaging and rewarding competition!

---

## 2. Long Detailed Version (For expansive forms or post-hackathon surveys)

### What the Hackathon Did Exceptionally Well:
1. **Practical, Problem-Centric Theme**: Instead of open-ended prompt engineering, the focus on persistent memory addressed one of the biggest genuine pain points in modern conversational AI — context loss between customer support sessions.
2. **Modern Developer Tooling**: Encouraging the integration of cutting-edge technologies like Hindsight Cloud for memory bank isolation and Groq for ultra-low latency inference gave participants exposure to modern AI infrastructure.
3. **Emphasis on Real-World Quality**: The challenge inspired us to focus not just on UI, but on backend architecture, token security, customer isolation, automated testing, and graceful degradation during service outages.

### Constructive Suggestions for Future Editions:
1. **Submission Format Clarity**: Announcing video submission length limits (e.g., 3 vs. 5 minutes) and preferred hosting services (YouTube Unlisted, Loom, or Google Drive) early in the event timeline helps teams pace their recording workflow.
2. **Judging Criteria Transparency**: A clear breakdown of weightage between UI/UX, backend engineering, memory retention effectiveness, and documentation provides valuable guidance during the final build phase.
3. *(Optional — Include only if applicable to your experience)*: Hosting an early Q&A or discord thread on third-party SDK rate limits and deployment tips for free cloud platforms (like Render or Vercel) would assist solo builders in avoiding common deployment bottlenecks.

Overall, HackwithHyderabad was a remarkably well-structured and intellectually challenging hackathon. Thank you to the organizing team for creating this opportunity!
